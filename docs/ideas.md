## Age of sail: a game mode chosen at the start of a new game

The same game on the same engine, set in the era of sailing ships (or the
sail-to-steam transition, roughly 1840–1880). Picked when starting a new game.

What carries over almost unchanged:
- Ports, goods, contracts, deadlines, the economy, events, the fleet and routes.
  The classic trades were sailing trades: tea from Canton, wool from Sydney,
  sugar from the Caribbean, timber from the Baltic.
- Wear and repairs: rot, shipworm and weed; careening instead of dry dock;
  copper sheathing as an upgrade.
- A ship ladder: sloop, schooner, brig, barque, full-rigged ship, East
  Indiaman, tea clipper.
- The port view: masts, rigging and furled sails at the quay.

What changes:
- **Wind instead of fuel.** Routes follow the trade winds, the westerlies and
  the monsoon, and voyage times depend on the season. The shortest route on the
  map is often not the fastest. This is the main new system (needs seasonal
  wind data on the sea-lane graph).
- **Crew and provisions instead of bunkers.** Wages, food and water set the
  range; scurvy on long legs, desertion in gold-rush ports, press gangs in war.
- **Slow news.** Prices seen in port are weeks or months old by the time the
  ship arrives.
- **Bigger events:** wars and blockades, privateers and pirates, convoys,
  insurance, chartered monopolies, no Suez Canal until 1869.
- **Speculation as an option:** buy the cargo yourself and sell it at the other
  end, besides carrying freight for a fee.
- **Pace:** voyages of 3–6 months, so a faster clock or fewer, bigger voyages.
- **Automation** reads as hiring a trusted captain rather than running a liner
  service.

A good compromise is the transition era: start under sail; steam and the Suez
Canal arrive during the game. Steamers need coal and coaling stations (fuel
comes back); sailing ships stay cheap and win on the longest hauls. Choosing
between old and new technology becomes the strategy.

Old port names for that era: Canton, Batavia, Bombay, Constantinople, Saigon.
